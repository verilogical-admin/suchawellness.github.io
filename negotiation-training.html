Negotiation Lab

Listen your way to a better deal.

A practical tool inspired by Chris Voss's negotiation principles in Never Split the Difference: calm the room, surface the real stakes, ask better questions, and make agreements people can execute.

Use these skills ethically. Negotiation is not manipulation; it is disciplined listening, clarity, and mutual reality-testing under pressure.

The goal is not to win the sentence. The goal is to discover the deal beneath the words.

Core model

The negotiation is emotional before it is logical.

People rarely move because you have a better argument. They move when they feel understood, safe enough to say the truth, and responsible for solving the problem with you.

Tactical empathy

Make the other person feel accurately heard.

This is not agreement. It is a temporary map of their world so you can negotiate with reality instead of your assumptions.

M

Mirror

Repeat one to three important words with a calm upward tone, then pause.

Them: "The deadline is impossible."
You: "Impossible?"

L

Label

Name the emotion, worry, or value you hear. Start gently.

"It sounds like you are worried this will create extra work."

S

Summary

Paraphrase their position until they feel seen enough to correct or confirm it.

"So the issue is not price alone; it is risk, timing, and trust."

No-oriented asks

Let "no" create safety.

A forced yes can be fake. A clean no can reveal the real objection and give the other person a sense of control.

Instead of chasing yes

"Would it be a bad idea to look at one option that reduces your risk?"

Useful when the other person feels pressured.

When they go silent

"Have you decided not to move forward with this?"

Often easier to answer than another cheerful follow-up.

When trust is low

"Is this too risky to consider right now?"

Let them correct the risk instead of defending your offer.

When you need truth

"Is there something about this that does not work for you?"

Respectful no-space protects both sides from fake agreement.

Calibrated questions

Ask questions that make the problem shared.

Use "how" and "what" to reveal constraints, priorities, and hidden information. Avoid questions that sound like cross-examination.

Execution and hidden information

A deal is not real until it can survive Monday.

Before you celebrate, check implementation. Who else matters? What could block this? What has not been said yet?

3

Rule of three

Get commitment in three different ways: agreement, next step, and practical confirmation.

  • "Does that work?"
  • "What happens next?"
  • "Who needs to approve it?"
B

Behind the table

Ask who influences the decision, who can block execution, and whose concerns are invisible.

X

Black swan hunt

Look for unknown information that could change everything: a hidden deadline, fear, incentive, rule, or stakeholder.
