Core model

Distraction is any action that pulls you away from what you intended.

The skill is not hating your phone. The skill is deciding what traction looks like first, then arranging your environment and emotions so the intended action becomes easier.

01

Internal triggers

Most distraction starts as discomfort: boredom, anxiety, loneliness, uncertainty, resentment, fatigue, or pressure.

02

Traction

Traction is action that moves you toward your values. If it is not planned, distraction wins by default.

03

External triggers

Notifications, open tabs, noisy rooms, and vague requests are easier to handle before they interrupt you.

04

Pacts

Commitments, friction, identity, and effort barriers protect focus after the plan is clear.

Internal triggers

The urge is data, not an order.

Before opening the app, tab, inbox, or snack, ask what discomfort wants relief.

Common urge translations

Boredom may mean the task needs a smaller first step.
Anxiety may mean the outcome feels too undefined.
Resentment may mean the task feels imposed or unfair.
Loneliness may turn checking into simulated connection.

The 10-minute ride

"I can do the distraction in ten minutes if I still want it. First, I ride the urge and take one tiny traction step."

This preserves choice. You are not forbidding the urge; you are delaying automatic obedience.

Timeboxing

Make your values visible on the calendar.

A to-do list stores guilt. A timebox stores intention. Give your day a shape before the world writes over it.

External triggers and pacts

Design the environment before relying on willpower.

Mute

Hack back triggers

Turn off nonessential notifications, close open loops, and make interruption a deliberate choice.

Friction

Effort pact

Put the distracting app behind a blocker, logout, or second device so impulse has to climb a small hill.

Name

Identity pact

Say: "I am someone who protects attention for what matters." Let identity reduce negotiation.
